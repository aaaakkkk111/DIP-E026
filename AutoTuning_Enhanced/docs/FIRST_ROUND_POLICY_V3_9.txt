

FIRST-ROUND DECISIVE CORRECTION
Read tuning_round.first_round and tuning_round.number. On the first round, when the current baseline shows sustained abnormal oscillation or sway and the live pattern plus conditional empirical experience support a direction, you may make a decisive large correction immediately. Do not require a preliminary tiny probe, a previous local trial, or repeated small percentage steps before choosing a meaningful corrective magnitude. An empty local_direction_summary on round one is expected, not evidence that a small step or hold is mandatory.
Choose direction first, then size the correction to the severity of the observed pattern and how strongly current conditions support the hypothesis. In the user's unloaded AP150/AD120/VP62/VI31/TP17/TD20 case with high osc and comparatively bounded angle, a substantial AD reduction may be the first corrective experiment; do not automatically reduce AD by only a token amount. Decide whether AP or VP/VI should change together from current lean, angle behavior and the empirical context. This is not a command to reset to AD48, a universal PID target, or a rule to lower AD in a low-AD large-angle failure.
All six gains may change together, including a large first-round move, within the existing absolute ranges. There is no required minimum or maximum percentage step, no new confidence cutoff, and no obligation to change all six. State the measured pattern, the correction direction and why the chosen magnitude is proportionate in expected_effect; acknowledge that the result is an unproven experiment. In optional independent review, lack of a prior local A/B trial or a large first-round magnitude alone is not a rejection reason when the present evidence supports a coherent hypothesis.
This permission does not force an aggressive move when the baseline is already stable or data is insufficient. The existing hold behavior, protocol, numeric bounds and measured acceptance/rollback rules remain unchanged. From the next round onward, use the actual response and local directional evidence to decide whether to continue, hold or revise; do not repeat a large move just because round one permitted it.
