These real-vehicle experiments were performed with NO PAYLOAD on LEVEL GROUND. Current payload and slope can differ, change during use, or be unknown. Never assume the historical conditions still hold. Different external conditions can require different gains; use this experience only as conditional directional evidence, not as standard values, universal thresholds, or a lookup controller.
Historical reference vector in AP/AD/VP/VI/TP/TD order: [96,48,62,31,17,20]. Each experiment below changed ONLY the named coefficient relative to this reference. The reference is an experimental control, not an instruction to reset the current controller.

Reference rechecks mostly said "no significant change" without numeric repeats. Battery varied between trials, particularly T6 at 12.30V versus the reference at 12.02V and other runs near 11.6V. Do not claim statistical significance, isolated causation, or a proven optimum.
Conditional use:
1. Near the reference conditions, AP56 worsened low-frequency sway, whereas AP150 reduced lean and visible travel without clear osc improvement. Poor restoring/low-frequency sway may justify exploring higher AP when live evidence supports it. Do not extrapolate that higher AP is always better, prescribe AP150, or attribute all low-frequency problems to AP.
2. Both AD29 and AD100 were worse than AD48 in these tests. High osc alone does not identify whether AD should rise or fall. Consider current AD, other coefficients and recent responses; an excessively low and an excessively high AD can require opposite experimental directions. AD48 is a tested reference, not a universally optimal damping value.
3. VP30 produced large fore-aft motion; VP94 produced much larger osc and lean despite about 2cm excursion. Do not keep raising VP solely to reduce travel. Consider a tradeoff between position behavior and posture oscillation, and do not conclude that VP94 is unsuitable under every load or slope.
4. VI1 improved the short observation, while VI100 worsened it. Reducing VI can be a hypothesis for similar slow hunting, but VI1 is not a long-term drift or payload/slope solution. Different sustained disturbances can require different integral action.
5. Single-parameter successes do not prove combined successes: AP150 plus VI1, or any other combination, was NOT tested. Explain coordinated changes as new hypotheses. These static tests provide no identified steering-gain preference.
